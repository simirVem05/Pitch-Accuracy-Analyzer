## 1. The core reframe

The project has been conflating two independent questions under one score:

| Axis | Question | Ground truth source |
|---|---|---|
| **Harmonic appropriateness** | Do the notes chosen fit the music? | The song's own instrumental |
| **Intonation precision** | Were those notes sung cleanly? | Cents deviation from a tuning-corrected target |

These are orthogonal. A singer can nail a deliberate ♭5 dead-center — *excellent*
intonation, *low* vertical consonance, musically superb. A singer can also drift
20 cents flat on a plain root note — *correct* note choice, *poor* execution. One
number cannot express either case honestly, and the current system's single
`on_key_score` collapses them into a value that means neither.

Splitting the axes is not just clearer reporting. It **dissolves the architectural
problem** that made the current system brittle: once "does this note fit" is a
continuous harmonic-salience question answered by the audio, there is nothing left
for a hand-maintained allowlist to decide.

## 2. Why the current system fails

Four failure modes, each traced to code.

### 2.1 The allowlist is unfalsifiable guesswork

`note_segmentation.py:57-65` hardcodes borrowed-note sets per genre:

```python
GENRE_EXTRA_INTERVALS = {
    "hip hop": {1, 6, 10},
    "pop": {8, 6},
    "r&b": {6, 1},
    ...
}
```

Three problems:

1. **No provenance.** Nothing documents where these intervals came from or how
   they were validated. They cannot be tuned against anything.
2. **Some are redundant.** `"country": {2, 3, 10}` — interval 2 is already
   diatonic in major, so that entry is partly a no-op.
3. **It answers the wrong question.** Genre does not determine which notes are
   correct in a *specific song*. Two R&B songs in B minor with different chord
   progressions have different correct-note sets. Genre is a weak prior over an
   enormous space.

The literature does not contain this approach (RESEARCH §7.2). Its absence is
informative: it does not work well enough to publish.

### 2.2 The rescue logic is compensating for the wrong model

`note_classification.py:115-135` adds contextual rescue — reclassifying
`dissonant` notes as `passing` / `neighbor` / `leading` from neighboring pitch
classes — plus a 65-cent near-miss window (`NEAR_ALLOWED_CENTS`).

This machinery exists to patch false positives generated by §2.1. The music
theory behind it is real (RESEARCH §6.1), but it is applied without the two
inputs that make non-chord-tone classification meaningful: **the sounding
harmony** and **beat position**. Per RESEARCH §6.1, whether a dissonance is a
suspension or a mistake is *defined* by strong-vs-weak beat placement. The current
code has no beat tracker, so it is guessing.

The result is a chain of if/else branches, each patching the previous one's
errors. Adding branches to fix the remaining cases is a losing strategy — your
own diagnosis, and the research supports it.

### 2.3 A440 is assumed, and it is measurably wrong

Nothing in the pipeline estimates tuning. `note_segmentation.py:18-27` hardcodes
the A440 equal-tempered grid:

```python
def hz_to_midi(hz): return int(np.round(12.0 * np.log2(hz / 440.0) + 69.0))
def midi_to_hz(midi): return 440.0 * (2.0 ** ((midi - 69) / 12.0))
```

Measured on this repo's own samples (RESEARCH §9.1), tuning offsets span
**−16 to +16 cents**. The scoring curve's entire "pro zone" is 0–25 cents
(`scoring.py:19`). **A 16-cent offset consumes 64% of that budget before the
singer sings a note** — and it applies uniformly, so a perfectly in-tune
performance over a sharp backing track reads as consistently sharp. This is
indistinguishable from real error (RESEARCH §4.2).

This is the single highest-value fix in the entire redesign, and the cheapest.

### 2.4 12-TET is the wrong target for the target genres

The scoring curve measures |cents| from the nearest equal-tempered semitone. Per
RESEARCH §5:

- A just major third sits **−13.7 ¢** from 12-TET.
- A just minor third sits **+15.6 ¢**.
- Leading tones get sharpened ~**+10 ¢** by skilled singers.
- Blue notes (♭3, ♭5, ♭7) are **deliberately microtonal** — "flattened by a
  variable microtone."

Measured median deviation on this repo's samples is **~22.5 cents** (RESEARCH
§9.2). A just major third alone accounts for 13.7 of those cents. The system
cannot currently distinguish "sang an expressive third" from "sang a sloppy
third," and R&B/soul/gospel — the stated target genres — are built precisely on
the intervals a 12-TET grid penalizes.

This explains the reported symptom: **well-sung songs not scoring well.**

## 3. What the research validates — and what it warns about

### Validated

**Hsieh et al., Interspeech 2025** (RESEARCH §7.1) implements the core idea —
pitch reference inferred from the accompaniment — and reports:

| Method | Pearson r vs. human raters |
|---|---|
| **Accompaniment-derived reference** | **0.611** |
| Pitch-interval, reference-free (Nakano-style) | 0.364 |
| Pitch-histogram, reference-free (Gupta-style) | 0.232 |

Deriving harmony from the instrumental nearly doubles agreement with human
judgment. And the space is almost empty: exhaustive DBLP queries for
singing-assessment × chord-detection return **zero** results (RESEARCH §7.2).

Note also that Hsieh et al. used **key** — a global property — not frame-level
harmony. A chord- or chroma-level approach is a step *beyond* published work.

### Three warnings

**1. The instrumental tells you note-fit, not note-quality.**
Zhang et al. (ISMIR 2021, RESEARCH §7.3) used the accompaniment as a
metric-learning anchor and it **underperformed**. Their finding: the
accompaniment "does not provide details on singing, but only helps with judging
the rhythm and tonality." This is the most important cautionary result in the
dossier, and it argues directly for the two-axis split in §6 — the instrumental
feeds the harmonic axis *only*.

**2. "Separate every instrument" is impossible.**
Production separation gives a fixed 4 stems; `other` stays polyphonic (RESEARCH
§2.1). There are no per-melodic-instrument stems to run CREPE on. The fix (§4)
is better than the original plan anyway.

**3. There is a ceiling.**
r = 0.611 is good, not solved. Quartal voicings have no definite root; pedal
points, chromatic mediants, and sustained-tension gospel technique all produce
notes that clash yet are correct (RESEARCH §6.3). Harmonic context cannot fully
determine correctness even when perfectly identified.

## 4. Proposed architecture

```
full mixed song (user upload)
  │
  ├─► Demucs htdemucs_ft ──► vocals / drums / bass / other
  │                            (MIT, 4 stems, ~15-30s GPU)
  │
  ├─ TUNING  ── estimate offset from (other + bass) ────────┐
  │             librosa.estimate_tuning                     │
  │                                                          │
  ├─ HARMONY ── chroma_cqt(other) + CREPE(bass) ──► time-varying
  │             12-dim pitch-class salience + root motion    │
  │             + beat/downbeat tracking                     │
  │                                                          │
  └─ VOCAL  ─── CREPE(vocals), confidence-gated ──► note segments
                                                             │
                                                             ▼
                        ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
                        │ AXIS 1: harmonic appropriateness   │
                        │   note pitch class vs. salience    │
                        │   at that time, ±1 beat slack      │
                        ├────────────────────────────────────┤
                        │ AXIS 2: intonation precision       │
                        │   cents from tuning-corrected      │
                        │   target (context-aware)           │
                        └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.1 Stems

`demucs` 4.1.0, model `htdemucs_ft`, **MIT-licensed including weights**
(RESEARCH §2.5). Consume three of four stems: `vocals` (the performance),
`other` (harmony), `bass` (roots). Ignore `drums`.

Runtime is the main UX cost: ~15–30 s on GPU, **3–8 min on CPU**. This forces a
decision about job handling (§10).

### 4.2 Tuning offset — do this first

`librosa.estimate_tuning` on the instrumental stems. Already installed, ~10 lines,
no new dependency. Subtract the offset from the reference grid before any cents
measurement.

Estimating from the **instrumental** rather than the voice is the correct
direction: fixed-pitch instruments define the grid the singer is aiming at
(RESEARCH §4.2). This is also an argument for the full-mix redesign — with only
an a cappella, there is nothing reliable to estimate against.

### 4.3 Harmony from chroma + bass, not per-stem CREPE

**Do not** separate-then-monophonically-track. Go straight to a polyphonic
representation:

- **`librosa.feature.chroma_cqt(other)`** → time-varying 12-dim pitch-class
  salience. Chords need no special handling because the representation never
  commits to a single note (RESEARCH §3.2). Output is a **continuous prior**, not
  an allowlist — this is what structurally removes the if/else problem.
- **CREPE on `bass`** → root motion. Bass is monophonic by construction, so this
  is the one place per-stem monophonic tracking is *correct*. Disambiguates
  inversions and rootless voicings chroma cannot resolve.
- **Beat/downbeat tracking** (`madmom`, or `librosa.beat`) → the strong/weak beat
  distinction that non-chord-tone classification requires (RESEARCH §6.1, §6.4).

Discrete chord labels are **optional** and belong in the *presentation* layer
("you sang a ♭9 over Cmaj7"), not the scoring path — ACE vocabularies flatten
extensions, and no benchmark on separated stems was obtained (RESEARCH §3.2
Tier 3).

Chroma is also robust to separation artifacts in a way vocal f0 is not: pooling
energy into 12 pitch classes tolerates spectral holes and moderate bleed
(RESEARCH §2.3).

## 5. The two polyphony problems

### 5.1 Chords in the instrumental — solved

Use chroma (§4.3). A C-major triad simply lights pitch classes 0, 4, 7. There is
no monophonic assumption to violate, so **chords stop being a problem rather
than needing a solution.** Bass CREPE supplies root disambiguation.

### 5.2 Harmonies in the vocal stem — the hard one

This is worse than it first appears. The `vocals` stem contains **lead + backing
+ harmonies + doubles + ad-libs** — every model, no exceptions — and no
production-quality lead/backing separator exists (RESEARCH §2.4). So on a
commercially produced track, CREPE receives *polyphonic* input and will follow
whichever voice is loudest per frame, **jumping between lead and harmony
mid-phrase**.

Four mitigations, ordered by how much I'd trust them:

1. **Confidence-gate hard, drop ambiguous frames.** Already partly implemented
   (`preprocess.py:20`, threshold 0.60). Scores fewer notes; does not score them
   wrong. Cheapest and most honest.
   Supporting method: Rosenzweig et al., ICASSP 2021 on f0 reliability via
   algorithm agreement (RESEARCH §7.7).
2. **Use predominant-melody extraction instead of CREPE where harmonies are
   present.** The PreFEst/Melodia lineage (Goto 2004) is purpose-built for
   extracting the leading line from polyphony — the right tool for this input
   (RESEARCH §3.4).
3. **Accept a dry lead vocal when the user has one.** Keeps the existing
   a cappella path as the *high-accuracy mode*. Best-of-both: full mix for
   harmony, clean vocal for intonation.
4. **Detect harmony presence and mark the section unscorable.** Honest
   degradation beats confident wrong numbers.

**Recommendation: (1) + (3) now, (2) if accuracy demands it.** Note the
asymmetry — separation damage barely affects the harmony path but directly
corrupts the intonation path, so the vocal input deserves the conservative
treatment.

## 6. Two-axis scoring

### Axis 1 — Harmonic appropriateness

For each sung note: compare its pitch class against the instrumental's
pitch-class salience *at that time*, with **±1 beat of slack** at chord
boundaries to accommodate anticipations (RESEARCH §6.4).

Properties that matter:

- **Continuous**, from measured salience — no allowlist, no genre table.
- **Song-specific** — derived from this song's actual harmony, not a genre prior.
- **Beat-aware** — weak-beat dissonance is penalized less than strong-beat
  dissonance, which is what the theory actually says (RESEARCH §6.1).

Delete `GENRE_EXTRA_INTERVALS` and the rescue chain entirely. Genre may survive
as a *weak* prior (e.g. blue-note tolerance in §Axis 2), not as a gate.

### Axis 2 — Intonation precision

Cents deviation from a target that is:

- **Tuning-corrected** — offset from §4.2 removed. Non-negotiable.
- **Context-aware** — optionally adapt the target per RESEARCH §5: thirds
  −13.7 ¢, leading tones +10 ¢, wider tolerance on blue-note degrees in
  blues-derived genres. Start simple; this is the part most likely to overfit
  (§10).

Keep the existing core-median trimming (`note_segmentation.py:136-162`) — it
correctly excludes scoops and releases, and it is well-motivated.

### Reporting

Report both axes plus a coverage figure (what fraction of the vocal was
confidently scorable). Do **not** average the axes into one number — that
recreates the problem this redesign exists to fix. If a single headline is needed
for UX, show the two side by side and let the report text explain the
interaction.

## 7. Build order

Each step is independently testable against the existing samples, and each
delivers value alone. Ordered by value-per-effort.

| # | Step | Effort | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tuning-offset estimation** | ~10 lines | nothing |
| 2 | **Test harness on pure functions** | small | nothing |
| 3 | Demucs integration, 4 stems | small | job handling (§10) |
| 4 | Chroma harmonic profile from `other` + bass CREPE | medium | 3 |
| 5 | Two-axis scoring; delete allowlist | medium | 4 |
| 6 | Beat/downbeat tracking + ±1 beat slack | medium | 4 |
| 7 | Harmony-aware vocal handling (§5.2) | large | 3 |
| 8 | Context-aware intonation targets | medium | 1, 5 |

**Steps 1 and 2 should happen regardless of whether the rest proceeds.** Step 1
fixes a measured bug in the current system. Step 2 exists because there is
currently no way to falsify any accuracy claim (§9).

Suggested first milestone: **1 + 3 + 4**, then compare the derived harmonic
profile against the existing allowlist on the bundled samples. That answers "does
instrumental-derived harmony actually disagree with genre guessing, and in the
right direction?" before committing to the full rewrite.

## 8. What to keep from the current system

The DSP core is sound. Do not rewrite these:

- **CREPE** for vocal f0 — correct choice, high accuracy for monophonic pitch
  (RESEARCH §2.3). **But raise `model_capacity` from `"tiny"`**
  (`preprocess.py:159`) — it is currently the smallest and least accurate
  variant, and `_run_preprocess`'s `inspect` indirection in `main.py:160-176`
  makes it unreachable to change from `run_backend`.
- **Voicing mask + morphological smoothing** (`preprocess.py:110-123`) — the
  fill-gaps → drop-short-runs → fill-gaps pipeline is well-built, and
  `NaN`-as-unvoiced is a clean cross-module contract.
- **Hysteresis note segmentation** (`note_segmentation.py:165`) — prevents jitter
  and spurious note flips. Genuinely good.
- **Core-median trimming** — excludes scoops/releases from the pitch center.
  Well-motivated and consistent with expressive-intonation findings.
- **Vibrato / portamento detection as reported-not-judged** — correct treatment
  of stylistic features; keep the philosophy.
- **Single `run_backend` shared by API and CLI** — good structure, keep it.
- **Gemini report as non-essential** — graceful degradation on API failure is
  already right (`main.py:144-157`).

The problem was never pitch *tracking*. It was the harmonic-correctness model
layered on top.

## 9. Honest limitations

State these plainly rather than discovering them later.

1. **There is no ground truth.** No labeled data, no test suite. Any claim the
   new system is "more accurate" is currently **unfalsifiable**. Hsieh et al. hit
   this same wall and resorted to commercial Tunebat key labels. Decide how
   correctness gets measured *before* building (§10).
2. **Ceiling ~r = 0.6, not 1.0.** Best published accompaniment-derived result.
   Quartal harmony, pedal points, chromatic mediants, and intentional sustained
   tension remain unresolvable (RESEARCH §6.3).
3. **Vocal harmonies degrade accuracy** on exactly the commercially-produced
   tracks users are most likely to upload (§5.2).
4. **CPU runtime is a real UX problem.** 3–8 min per song on CPU, and the current
   `/analyze` endpoint blocks the entire event loop (`api.py:27`, declared
   `async def` while doing synchronous CPU work).
5. **Separation artifacts are borderline for f0.** `htdemucs_ft` vocals ≈ 9.4 dB
   SDR against a ~10 dB literature threshold for reliable pitch tracking
   (RESEARCH §2.3). Unmeasured on this repo's material — worth quantifying early.
6. **Section 3 of RESEARCH.md is the weakest-sourced part** of the dossier (the
   assigned agent never reported). Version-check madmom/Basic Pitch/Chordino
   before depending on them.
7. **This plan is unimplemented and unvalidated.** It is a research-backed
   proposal, not a tested design.

## 10. Decisions you need to make

1. **Ground truth strategy.** Hand-label a small set of your own recordings?
   Rank-order performances by ear and check correlation? Use an existing dataset
   (DAMP, VocalSet — RESEARCH §7.8)? **Without this, step 5 onward cannot be
   evaluated.** This is the most important open decision.
2. **Full mix vs. a cappella.** Support both (full mix for harmony, optional dry
   vocal for intonation), or require full mix? Recommendation: both — it makes
   the existing path the high-accuracy mode rather than dead code.
3. **Job handling for Demucs.** 3–8 min CPU runtime is incompatible with a
   blocking HTTP request. Options: background job + polling, GPU host, or
   accept long waits and at minimum stop blocking the event loop.
4. **How far to push context-aware intonation targets** (§Axis 2). Principled per
   RESEARCH §5, but the most overfit-prone part of the plan. Recommendation:
   ship tuning correction first, measure, then decide.
5. **Chord labels in the UI?** Adds interpretability and a failure mode. Scoring
   does not need them.
6. **Does genre survive at all?** It could remain a weak prior for blue-note
   tolerance. Or drop it entirely and let the audio speak.
